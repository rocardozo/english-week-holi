The Story of Holi ✨

Why do millions of people celebrate the Festival of Colours across the world?

🌸
Introduction

What is Holi?

Holi is an ancient Hindu festival from India. People celebrate it every year in March during the full moon to welcome the arrival of Spring 🌿. It is known as the Festival of Colours and represents love, joy, new beginnings, and unity.

Ancient Legend

The Legend of Prahlada & Holika

Long ago in India, there was a powerful and cruel king named Hiranyakashipu. He demanded that everyone worship him as a god. But his young son, Prince Prahlada, was kind and refused. He worshiped the protector god, Lord Vishnu.

⚡ The Fire Trial

The furious king ordered his demon sister, Holika, to destroy Prahlada. Holika had a magical shawl that made her immune to fire. She carried young Prahlada into a roaring bonfire.

✨ But a miracle happened! Prahlada's pure devotion made the magical shawl fly off Holika to cover Prahlada. Holika burned, and Prahlada was saved!

🌟 Moral: Good always triumphs over evil!

🗓️
Festival Schedule

Why is Holi celebrated over 2 Days?

Holi is not just a single morning; it has two very distinct, meaningful parts:

🔥 Day 1: Holika Dahan (Chhoti Holi) Night

On the first evening, people gather dry wood and build big bonfires in streets and squares. At night, they light the fire to remember the story of Prahlada. People sing, pray, and walk around the fire. It symbolizes burning away negativity, fear, and bad feelings.

🎨 Day 2: Rangwali Holi (Dhulandi) All Day

The next morning is the main party! People wear white clothes and run outdoors with bright coloured powders (Gulal), water guns (pichkaris), and water balloons. Everyone dances to drums (dhol), laughs, and paints each other's faces.

🗣️ Famous phrase: “Bura na mano, Holi hai!” (Don't be upset, it's Holi!)

Flavours & Rituals

Traditional Food & Friendship

After playing with colours, people take a bath, put on clean clothes, and visit relatives and neighbors to share sweet treats:

🥟

Gujiya

Delicious fried sweet dumplings filled with milk fudge (khoya), raisins, and almonds.

🥛

Thandai

A refreshing cold drink made of milk, crushed almonds, rose water, and spices.
